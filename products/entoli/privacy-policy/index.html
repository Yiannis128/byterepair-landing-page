Privacy Policy

Entoli is a chat harness for coding agents and language models on your device. This policy describes what the app stores and what leaves your device.

Data stored on your device

The app stores its data locally under its private data directory:

  • Conversations — your messages, the agent’s replies, tool calls and their results, and files you attach.
  • Settings — the LLM providers you configure, including their API keys, the models you pick, the theme, and command environment variables.
  • Memory — facts the agent extracts from conversations to recall in later ones, and the instruction files you write.
  • Workspace files — files the agent creates or edits while running commands.
  • Reminders — the name, time, and prompt of each reminder you set.
  • Agent browser profile (desktop) — the cookies, logins, history and site data of the browser the agent drives, kept in the app’s cache directory, apart from your own browser’s profile.

None of this leaves your device except as described below. Uninstalling the app removes all of it, except on desktop, where the data and cache directories stay until you delete them. The agent browser’s profile can be deleted from Settings > Agent Browser > Browser data. Backups are user-initiated exports to a local file the user chooses, and do not include the browser profile.

Data sent to third parties

The app has no server of its own. No analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting runs in the app, and no data is sent to a server controlled by the app.

The following third parties receive data when you use the relevant features:

LLM providers you configure

When you send a message, the app calls the LLM provider you have configured directly. It sends the conversation as the model sees it: your messages, attachment contents, tool results, recalled memories, and the system prompt. The provider’s own privacy policy applies to the data they receive. The app calls their endpoint directly; there is no proxy or intermediary controlled by the app.

Model catalog

When at least one hosted LLM provider is configured, the app fetches the public model list from models.dev (https://models.dev/api.json) at most once a day, to know each model’s context window, price and what it can read. The request is a plain GET carrying your IP address and standard HTTP headers; nothing about you or your conversations is sent.

Web search

The agent’s web search queries DuckDuckGo directly from your device over HTTPS.

The agent browser

On desktop, you can give the agent a browser to drive: the embedded Helium or a Chromium-family browser you pick. It is off until you pick one. While the agent uses it:

  • The sites it opens receive requests directly from your device, carrying your IP address, standard HTTP headers, and the cookies of the agent browser’s own profile, under each site’s own privacy policy.
  • The text of the pages it reads and the screenshots it takes are sent to your LLM provider as tool results, like any other tool’s output.
  • A file uploaded to a page is sent to that site. Depending on the upload setting, the classifier model sees the file’s path before it goes.
  • What you type into the sign-in prompt goes from the app to the page and nowhere else. It is not sent to your LLM provider, not saved in the conversation, and not synced. While a sign-in is in effect, it is removed from the page text the agent reads, and screenshots of the site you signed into are refused.

Model, voice, browser, and Linux downloads

Downloading an on-device model, a voice model, the embedded browser, or a Linux rootfs makes a request to the host that serves the file: Hugging Face for models and voice recognition models, GitHub for voice synthesis models and the Helium browser, and the Alpine Linux CDN for the rootfs. The request includes your IP address and standard HTTP headers, under the host’s own privacy policy. The files are stored on your device; inference and voice processing happen locally.

Marketplace

Browsing the marketplace fetches its catalog and entry files from GitHub Pages (byterepair.github.io), and the files of a remote entry from raw.githubusercontent.com. The requests are plain GETs carrying your IP address and standard HTTP headers, under GitHub’s own privacy policy. Installing an entry stores its files on your device; nothing else is sent.

Marketplace entries are the work of their publishers, not of the app’s authors, and installing one means using it under the licence its publisher assigned. Entries the site publishes itself are AGPL-3.0; external entries keep whatever their own authors gave them, and the app does not verify licences — a skill shows one only where its SKILL.md names it.

Android speech recognition and text-to-speech

Voice mode on Android uses the operating system’s speech recognition and text-to-speech services. These are OS services you choose. Depending on the recognizer, audio may be processed on a server by that service’s operator, under that service’s own privacy policy. The app prefers offline recognition when an offline engine is available.

Sync

Sync pairs two of your devices over an encrypted peer-to-peer connection. The data transmitted is the conversation: messages, attachments, and metadata.

Sync traffic is end-to-end encrypted with ChaCha20-Poly1305 AEAD layered over WebRTC DTLS. The encryption keys are derived from a pairing secret you exchange out-of-band and never sent to any server.

Pairing rendezvous goes through a relay URL you supply (or over your local network). The relay sees only opaque ciphertext and WebRTC signaling blobs; it cannot read the conversation. If you use a TURN relay for network traversal, it also sees only encrypted traffic.

On-device model

The optional bundled model (llama.cpp) runs entirely on your device. Its server binds to localhost and makes no network calls.

Permissions

The app requests the following permissions, each used only for its feature:

  • Microphone — voice mode listening.
  • Speech recognition (iOS) — hearing voice mode, on the device only.
  • Camera — scanning a sync pairing QR code.
  • Local network (iOS) — syncing with your other devices on the same network, and reaching a model or service you run on it.
  • Notifications — announcing reminders and finished turns.
  • Exact alarms (Android) — firing reminders on time.
  • Boot-completed (Android) — re-arming reminders after device restart.
  • Foreground service (Android) — keeping the process alive while a turn runs, while voice mode is active, or while a model downloads.
  • Internet — calling your configured LLM provider, web search, the agent browser, the marketplace, and sync.

No permission collects data in the background.

Children

The app is not intended for use by children. It is recommended that a parent or guardian supervise any use by a minor.
